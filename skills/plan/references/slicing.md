# Slicing the work into units

The method's default for any domain. When a package brings its own way of slicing (a
chapter per unit, a feature per unit), these rules still hold around it.

## What makes a good unit

- **It belongs to one part** of the definition, when the work has parts: a unit never
  mixes two.
- **It delivers whole capabilities:** one per unit, or a few small ones of the same part;
  never a layer or a step of one (not "the database", "the screens"). Its done criterion
  cites their acceptance criteria (`01-tasks/02-edit AC-01–03`) instead of copying them.
- **It leaves something that can be checked on its own:** a draft, a list, a decision, a
  booked date, a working change. Never "progress on X".
- **It fits one stop:** done, verified and reported in one sitting. If it needs two
  sittings, it is two units.
- **Its done criterion fits in one or two lines** and follows from the work's criterion,
  or from the criteria of its capabilities.
- **One who-does-it:** the agent, the person, or both. If the agent prepares and the person
  acts (book the movers, give the class), it is "both", and the unit says who does what.
- **Named in the person's words**, the ones in the glossary if there is one.

## How many

| Size | Units |
|---|---|
| planned | A few (about two to five), approved once; the person can take them as one batch |
| sliced | As many as the work needs. Plan in detail only the next few; later ones can stay as one line each until they are near |

If the count is far outside this, the size is probably wrong: say so and propose the other.

## Order

1. **What removes the biggest unknown or risk** (a decision everything depends on, a date
   that must be booked early, a prototype that may fail).
2. **Then what depends on it.**
3. **Under a fixed deadline, cuttable units last,** so that cutting them never undoes work.

## Batches

Units that are small, low-risk and done by the agent can run in a row when the person
approves them together. The agent still stops at the end of the batch, and stops earlier
if a unit fails or the session is about to run out of context.

## Changing the plan

A unit that turns out bigger is split; two tiny ones are merged; a new need becomes a new
unit. Every change is shown to the person before it is applied, and recorded in
*Decisions* with its reason.
