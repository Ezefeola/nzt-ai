# Exercises: the brief now, the solution afterwards

In a topic, produces `exercises/<number>-<slug>.md` and, later,
`solutions/<number>-<slug>.md`: same name, different folder. Numbers run in order per topic
and are never reused. In the conversation, the brief is part of the message and the
solution is not written yet. Every brief serves one objective and one step of the lesson;
if you cannot name them, there is nothing to correct against.

## The brief

| Part | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **What you hand back** | concretely, what they produce |
| **The case** | their own work, data or situation; one case per brief |
| **What you get** | what comes with it at this step, and **what does not**, said out loud |
| **Not today** | what is not practised, so they do not fix it and you do not correct it |
| **It is right when** | what has to be true of a good answer, not the answer, visible to the person |

- **What you get** follows the step: worked example, half done or on your own.
- Say how long it should take; an exercise with no size gets postponed.
- A practice session gets one brief for the whole series: it names the one part practised
  and says the rest is not looked at. Each repetition changes the case, not the brief.

An example, for a person learning Italian:

```markdown
# 08 · Tell what you did last weekend
Step: half done · about 15 minutes

## What you hand back
Five sentences about last weekend, in the past tense.

## The case
Your trip to the coast: the train, lunch with your sister, the walk on the beach.

## What you get
- The verbs you need, in the infinitive: prendere, pranzare, camminare, tornare, essere.
- Which ones go with *essere* and which with *avere*: **not given**. That is the exercise.

## Not today
Accents and word order. We look only at the helper verb and the ending.

## It is right when
- Every verb uses the right helper, *essere* or *avere*.
- With *essere*, the ending agrees with who did it.
```

## The solution

1. **Write it only once the delivery exists**, or once the person asked for it (recorded as
   `done with help`), never in the brief, in a hidden block or in advance: a solution that
   exists gets read, and one written early fixes your answer before you have seen theirs.
2. It carries the reasoning, why each decision and what the alternatives cost, not only the
   result.
3. When their answer differs and is also right, say so, with what each version gives up.

## Done when

The person could start from the brief without asking a question, and you could judge a
delivery from *It is right when* alone, without a solution written beforehand.
