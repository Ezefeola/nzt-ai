# Writing any document

The default for every document the work produces: the method's own (plan, stop report,
state) and the deliverables (a chapter, a spec, a syllabus). A package in use can add to or
replace the form of a deliverable of its domain; otherwise this applies.

## Before writing

1. **Know for whom and for what:** who reads it and what they come to do with it — decide,
   learn, carry out, look up. If it is unclear, ask.
2. **One document, one purpose.** If it serves two different things, it is two documents.
3. **Look for an existing one first.** Update it instead of creating a duplicate.

## How it is organized

4. **The important part first:** the answer, the decision or the result at the top; the
   detail after.
5. **Organized by what the reader came to do,** not by how the work was done.
6. **Length in proportion:** what fits in one paragraph gets no sections.
7. **Status and date visible** in the documents the method keeps for the work (a plan, a
   report, a spec): draft, in review or approved, and when it last changed. Not in the
   person's own texts (a letter, a message, a chapter of their book) nor in files that
   already have a form of their own.

## How it is written

8. **In the person's and the reader's language and words,** consistent with
   `Docs/glossary.md`, without the agent's jargon.
9. **Short sentences, one idea each,** and a concrete example where something is abstract.
10. **What is, apart from what is believed:** verified facts, assumptions and proposals are
    never mixed, and proposals are marked.
11. **Decisions with their reason.**
12. **Markdown by default**, because it reads as text and as a page. Another format (a Word
    document, a slide deck) when the person asks or the reader needs it.

## How it is checked

13. **Reread it as the reader:** do they find what they came for without reading it all? Is
    there anything they would not understand without having been in the conversation? If
    it matters, have someone without the conversation's context review it — another person,
    or a subagent.
