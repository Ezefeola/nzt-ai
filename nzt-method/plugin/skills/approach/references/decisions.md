# Finding the decisions

The decisions come from what the work is, never from a fixed list: a task app and a
birthday party share none. This table is a guide to start from; a work that fits no row,
or several, gets its decisions worked out the same way.

## What kind of work it is

| The work, or the part, is | Its decisions usually are |
|---|---|
| **Something built that runs** (an app, a game, a website, an automation, a spreadsheet with formulas) | where it runs (device, browser, server), the language or tool it is made with, where its data is kept, how it is checked, how it reaches whoever uses it |
| **Content** (a book, a course, a newsletter, a video) | its format, what it is written or recorded with, where it is published, the style it follows, how it is reviewed |
| **Something done in the world** (a move, an event, a renovation, a trip) | suppliers or who does each part, materials, the place, the budget split, the logistics (dates, transport, order) |
| **Research or an analysis** | the sources, the method, the tools to analyse with, the form of the result |

## Which ones to ask

- **Only the ones that are costly to change later** or that the person cares about. A detail
  that is cheap to change is decided by the agent inside what was agreed, said as the
  agent's.
- **Each one in its own question,** never folded into a question about what the work must
  do.
- **A choice already made by the evidence** (the project already uses it, the person
  already hired someone) is recorded, not asked.
- **What the person has and knows counts:** an option they already have installed, paid
  for or know how to use is worth saying so.

## How each option is written

- **What would be used, by its real name:** ".NET 10 with SQLite", "a moving company with a
  truck and three people", "a shared Google Drive folder".
- **What it means for the person:** what it costs, what they have to do or install, what
  they can and cannot do later ("the tasks stay on this laptop only").
- **The one you recommend first,** marked, with the reason in a few words.
- **For a person who does not know the field,** plain words first and the name after:
  "a page that opens in the browser (HTML and JavaScript)".
