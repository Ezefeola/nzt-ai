# Interviewing the person

Understanding comes before anything is proposed. The interview finds what the person needs,
which is often more than, or different from, what they first asked for.

## The starting questions

The default for any domain; a package in use may add its own. For the whole work, these
are what the general definition needs.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What should exist or change when this is over, and what problem does it solve? | It is the work itself, and its reason |
| For whom, and what will they do with it? How do they manage today? | It decides form, depth and tone |
| What is in, and what is clearly out? | It keeps the work from growing silently |
| What parts does it split into? | Each part is defined when its turn comes |
| By when, and how much is it worth to the person? | It decides the size and whether time is fixed |
| What already exists, and what limits apply (money, time, rules)? | They cut options before any is proposed |
| How well must it work: how many, how fast, how long it lasts? | It becomes *How well*, with numbers |
| How will we know it went well? | It becomes the done criterion |

Each one is asked, read from what the person already said, or said in a line not to
apply; none is skipped in silence.

For a part, the same questions narrow to that part, plus:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What can be done or achieved in it? | Each answer is a capability, with its own criteria |
| What is always true in it, whatever is being done? | They are its rules |
| What does it keep or deal with, and within which limits: what is required, the most and the least, which values are allowed, what if it is repeated? | They are *What it handles*; a limit left unasked turns up as a bug or a surprise |

## How to ask

1. **In what it does, never in how it is made.** If a question cannot be asked without a
   technology, a supplier or a material, it belongs to `approach`: note it there.
2. **In rounds,** the question that unblocks most first; never one question per turn,
   never twenty at once.
3. **A concrete case instead of a category:** "If you add *pay rent* on the 1st and do not
   tick it, what should happen on the 2nd?" rather than "how do you handle deadlines?".
   Every round, the first one too, has at least one.
4. **The edges early:** who else is affected, what happens before and after.
5. **The bad case by its name:** what happens when it goes wrong, is late, is empty, is
   done twice.
6. **Bring a proposal when the person may not know,** as a choice.
7. **Follow up an answer that opens something,** in the next round.
8. **Limits by their number:** "how long can a task's text be?", "how many guests at
   most?", with a proposed value the person can accept.

## When the person does not know yet

If the person is still looking for what they want (an idea, a creative search), propose a
short exploration first: a round of options or research whose only output is a clearer
request. Exploring is direct work done by `do`.
