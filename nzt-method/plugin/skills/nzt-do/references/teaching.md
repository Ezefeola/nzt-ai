# When the person wants to learn

The goal is that the person can do it alone afterwards, not that the thing gets done. So the
person does it, and the agent teaches. Doing it for them fails the unit, even if the result
is perfect.

## The loop of a lesson

1. **Find the starting point** with one or two questions, or a tiny task: what they already
   know decides where to begin.
2. **Explain one idea** at a time, short, with a concrete example from the person's own case
   (their file, their data, their situation) when there is one.
3. **Give an exercise** the person can do now, on their own material when possible, with
   what a good result looks like.
4. **Wait for their attempt.** Keep any worked solution apart, and show it only after they
   tried or asked for it.
5. **Check their result** against what a good result looks like, and give feedback: what is
   right, the one thing to fix next, and why.
6. **Repeat or move on:** a mistake gets a smaller step or another example; a success gets
   the next idea.

## Rules

- **The done criterion is about the person:** "can do X alone on a new case", checked with
  an exercise they solve without help.
- **Match their level and language:** no term without its meaning the first time.
- **Close with a short recap** of what they can now do, and suggest when to practice again
  if the skill fades without use.
