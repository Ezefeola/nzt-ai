# When the person does the work

Some work only the person can do: sign, pay, call, carry boxes, give the class. The agent
still owns the quality of the unit: it prepares, guides and verifies, and never reports as
done what it did not see.

## Split it explicitly

For "both", say before starting who does each part: "I draft the message, you send it".
The person's part is a short, concrete list of actions, never "handle the rest".

## Prepare

Leave the person everything that makes their part quick and hard to get wrong: a checklist
in order, a draft ready to send, the numbers to call, the questions to ask, what to bring.
Put it where the person will look (the work's folder, or the message itself). Prepare it
now, with blanks like `[floor]` for what is still missing: material the person can start
with beats a list of questions.

## Guide

- One step at a time when the steps depend on each other; the whole list when they do not.
- Say what a good result looks like at each step, so the person can tell on their own.
- When something goes off script (a supplier says no, a date moves), adapt the steps, and
  if it changes the plan, say so: changing the plan is `nzt-plan`'s.

## Verify with evidence

The agent cannot see the world; the person can show it. Say from the start which evidence
will close the unit, and ask for it when the person's part is done: a confirmation email, a photo, a receipt, a pasted result, a yes to a
precise question ("Is the booking for the 15th at 9 a.m., in writing?").

- Until the evidence arrives, the unit is not done: its verdict is *partial*, with what is
  missing, and the next step is the person's.
- A vague "done" is followed by the one precise question that would confirm it.
